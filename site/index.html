One evidence store, four front ends

Reviews, questions and proposed tests about Mathlib's probability theory live in one place: the evidence store of this repository, as records in git. Each front end below reads the same records against the same dataset of Mathlib, so a review written once shows up in all four, with its status against the current code.

Referee site

Mathlib's probability theory read as a referee does: claims, what they rest on, every declaration with its reviews, and coverage under the reader's choice of whose reviews count.

trust's front end

The dependency tree of a declaration, with what reviewers accepted marked as trusted and what changed since a review marked as such.

One claim's page

The strong law of large numbers: the statement, what it rests on, and every review, question and reply about those declarations, as threads.

Reviewed-by

Every declaration of Mathlib's probability theory, searchable, with its review marks and tests, and a form to add one from the browser.

Add to it

Anyone with a GitHub account can review a declaration, report a problem, ask a question or propose a test, with the issue forms (or the buttons on each front end). A bot turns the issue into a record, and the four front ends are rebuilt. Records are never anonymous: each names the GitHub account it came from, and an AI agent's are labelled as such.

How it is made

Each piece is open, in its own repository of LeanTrustBuilders, and they fit together through three specifications, so that any of them can be replaced by another tool that follows them.

  1. Mathlib, read by Lean. The extractor (trust-extract) reads the compiled library at one commit into a dataset: every declaration, with hashes of what it means, the graphs of what each one rests on, and facets such as docstrings, statements taken apart and where each is written. It can also have Lean's kernel check each declaration against only what the graph says it rests on. Mathlib Explorer, a site for reading Mathlib, extracts a dataset for each Mathlib release and keeps it as a release of its repository; this demo uses those. The Mathlib catalogue, a Lean package that states the domains and characterizations of Mathlib's definitions from outside Mathlib, has a dataset of its own, merged into Mathlib's.
  2. The specifications. specs: S1 names a declaration as it was (its name at a commit, and hashes of its meaning), S2 is the dataset, and S3 the evidence records and stores, with the rubric a review says what it checked against.
  3. The evidence. The records are the files of evidence/ in this repository. evidence-store turns the issues opened with the forms into records, keyed by the declaration's hashes in the dataset, and checks every change to the store. evidence-core reads the records against the dataset: whether each still applies to the current code, what changed underneath it, whether a claim is covered under a reader's policy, and what to review next.
  4. The front ends. referee-site builds the Referee site and the claim's page, and the index that trust's front end (trust-web) reads; Reviewed-by builds its page from the same dataset and store. This repository's Pages workflow builds all four after every new record.

The design behind the suite is in LeanTrustBuilders/design.
